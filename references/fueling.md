## Fueling by Session Duration

| Session Duration | Intensity | During-Ride Carb Target | Source | Notes |
|-----------------|-----------|------------------------|--------|-------|
| <45 min | Any | None needed | Water only | Pre-ride meal sufficient |
| 45-60 min | Recovery/Z2 | None needed | Water or electrolyte | Pre-ride meal sufficient |
| 45-60 min | Threshold/VO2max | 20-30g/h | Sports drink | Supports high-intensity execution |
| 60-90 min | Any structured | 30-60g/h | Sports drink, gel, or banana | Single carb source (glucose) sufficient |
| 90-120 min | Any | 60-80g/h | Glucose:fructose mix (~2:1) | Multiple transportable carbs increase absorption |
| >120 min | Any | 60-90g/h | Glucose:fructose mix (~2:1) | Gut-trained athletes can push toward 90g/h |

**Evidence basis**: Jeukendrup (2014, *Sports Medicine*) established that single-source carbohydrate maxes out at ~60g/h due to intestinal transporter saturation. Multiple transportable carbohydrates (glucose + fructose via different transporters) allow higher absorption rates up to ~90-108g/h. The 120g/h figures cited in pro cycling (Pogacar, EF Pro Cycling) come from field practice, not controlled studies — formal evidence supports up to ~90-108g/h with trained gut.

## Pre-Ride Nutrition

### Timing

| Time Before Ride | What to Eat | Serving Size |
|-----------------|-------------|-------------|
| 2-3 hours | Full meal: carb-rich, moderate protein, low fat/fiber | 1-2g carb/kg body weight |
| 1-2 hours | Light snack: toast, banana, rice cake | 0.5-1g carb/kg |
| 30-60 min | Small simple carb: banana, sports drink | 20-30g carb |
| <30 min | Avoid solid food | Sip water/sports drink |

### Key Rules

- **Low fiber pre-ride** for threshold/VO2max sessions — fiber increases GI risk during high-intensity work
- **Low fat** within 2 hours of hard sessions — fat slows gastric emptying
- **Familiar foods only** before key sessions — don't experiment on important training days
- **Caffeine** (3-6mg/kg, 30-60 min before): ergogenic for threshold/VO2max work; can increase GI urgency — test individually

### For the Current Athlete (70kg)

| Timing | Carb Target | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| 2-3h before | 75-150g | Bowl of rice/oats with banana, toast with honey |
| 1-2h before | 37-74g | 2 slices toast with jam, banana |
| 30-60 min before | 20-30g | Banana or sports drink |

## During-Ride Carbohydrate Targets

### Carbohydrate Sources

| Source | Carb Content | Absorption | Best For |
|--------|-------------|-----------|----------|
| Sports drink (6-8% solution) | 30-40g per 500ml | Fast | Sessions <90 min; easy to consume on trainer |
| Energy gel | 20-30g per gel | Fast | Mid-interval top-up |
| Banana | ~25g per medium | Moderate | Endurance rides; pre-interval fuel |
| Rice cake / bar | 30-40g each | Moderate-slow | Long rides; solid option for outdoor |

### Multiple Transportable Carbohydrates

For sessions >90 min or when targeting >60g/h:

- **Glucose:fructose ratio**: ~2:1 or ~1:0.8 — uses separate intestinal transporters (SGLT1 for glucose, GLUT5 for fructose)
- Most commercial sports nutrition already uses this ratio
- **Maltodextrin + fructose** is equivalent to glucose + fructose for this purpose
- Check ingredient labels: "maltodextrin, fructose" or "glucose syrup, fructose" indicates dual-source

### During-Ride Timing

- **Start fueling early** — don't wait until you feel depleted
- For structured sessions: sip sports drink during warm-up and between intervals
- For endurance rides: start carbohydrate intake within first 20-30 minutes
- Consume in small, frequent amounts (every 15-20 min) rather than large boluses

## Post-Ride Recovery Nutrition

### Recovery Window

| Timing | Priority | Target |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Within 30 min | Carbohydrate + protein | 0.8-1.2g carb/kg + 0.3-0.4g protein/kg |
| Within 2 hours | Full meal | Balanced meal with carb, protein, fat |

### For the Current Athlete (70kg)

| Component | Target | Example |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Carb (immediate) | 60-90g | Recovery shake, rice + fruit, 2 bananas + yogurt |
| Protein (immediate) | 22-30g | Protein shake, Greek yogurt, chicken breast |
| Full meal (within 2h) | Balanced | Rice/pasta + protein + vegetables |

### Recovery Priority by Session Type

| Session Type | Recovery Urgency | Notes |
|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| Recovery ride | Low | Normal meal timing sufficient |
| Endurance Z2 | Moderate | Refuel within 2 hours |
| Sweet spot / Threshold | High | Glycogen depleted; refuel within 30 min |
| VO2max | High | High carb demand; refuel within 30 min |
| Back-to-back days | Very high | Aggressive refueling to restore glycogen before next session |

## Gut Training Protocol

The gut is trainable — regular practice with carbohydrate intake during exercise increases intestinal absorption capacity and reduces GI distress.

### Progression

| Week | During-Ride Intake | Notes |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| 1-2 | 20-30g/h | Sports drink only; note any GI symptoms |
| 3-4 | 30-40g/h | Add gel if tolerating drink well |
| 5-6 | 40-60g/h | Introduce dual-source carbs if targeting higher intake |
| 7-8 | 60-80g/h | Multiple transportable carbs; race-day simulation |
| 9+ | 60-90g/h | Maintain or push based on tolerance and session demands |

### Rules

- **Only increase intake by 10-15g/h per 1-2 weeks** — don't rush
- **Practice on training rides, not key sessions** — use endurance days to experiment
- **If GI distress occurs**: drop back to previous tolerated level for 1 week, then retry
- **Track what works**: note exact products, timing, and amounts in session notes
- **Individual variation is large** — some athletes tolerate 90g/h easily; others plateau at 60g/h. Both are fine.

## GI Troubleshooting

### Common Causes of GI Distress During Cycling

| Cause | Mechanism | Solution |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Eating too close to session** | Stomach still processing; blood diverted to muscles during exercise | Move pre-ride meal to 2-3h before; light snack only within 1h |
| **High fiber pre-ride** | Fiber stimulates bowel; combined with intensity → urgency | Low-fiber meals before threshold/VO2max sessions |
| **High fat pre-ride** | Fat slows gastric emptying; causes nausea at high intensity | Low-fat within 2h of hard sessions |
| **Intensity-driven redistribution** | Threshold+ intensity diverts blood from gut to muscles → gut motility changes | Fuel before intensity begins; sip small amounts during rest intervals |
| **Dehydration** | Reduces gut blood flow; concentrates GI contents | Pre-hydrate; sip during session; monitor urine color |
| **Overhydration** | Excessive fluid causes bloating and sloshing | Don't force fluid; drink to thirst |
| **Caffeine sensitivity** | Stimulates gut motility; increases urgency | Reduce dose or eliminate; try after initial warm-up instead of before |
| **Untrained gut** | Low absorption capacity; carb sits in gut fermenting | Follow gut training progression above |
| **Specific food intolerance** | Lactose, fructose, FODMAPs, artificial sweeteners | Identify trigger via elimination; switch products |

### Threshold/VO2max-Specific GI Issues

High-intensity sessions (threshold and above) carry higher GI risk due to gut blood flow redistribution. Practical mitigations:

1. **Pre-ride timing**: 2-3 hours before threshold/VO2max sessions; low fiber, low fat
2. **Warm-up fueling**: Sip sports drink during warm-up (before intensity begins); gut absorbs better at lower intensities
3. **Between-interval fueling**: Small sips during recovery intervals, not during work intervals
4. **Pre-emptive bathroom**: Use the bathroom 15-30 min before session start; caffeine can help timing if tolerated
5. **Track the pattern**: If GI issues recur at specific intensity/duration thresholds, note the conditions (food, timing, hydration, stress) for systematic troubleshooting

## Hydration Guidelines

### General Targets

| Condition | Fluid Intake | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Indoor (fan, A/C) | 500-750ml/h | Higher sweat rate indoors due to lack of convective cooling |
| Indoor (no fan) | 750-1000ml/h | Dehydration risk high; always use a fan |
| Outdoor (temperate) | 400-600ml/h | Adjust for temperature and humidity |
| Outdoor (hot/humid) | 600-900ml/h | Consider sodium supplementation |

### Indoor-Specific Notes

- **Fan is essential** — indoor cycling without adequate cooling dramatically increases sweat rate and RPE
- **Pre-session hydration**: 250-500ml water 1-2 hours before
- **During session**: Drink to thirst; have 750ml-1L available for a 60-min session
- **Electrolytes**: Add electrolyte tablet or pinch of salt to water for sessions >60 min; sodium supports fluid retention

## Fasted Training

**Evidence summary**: Fasted training does NOT improve performance. Van Proeyen et al. (2011) showed identical VO2max gains (+9%) and time-trial improvements (+8%) in both fasted and fed groups. While fasted training may upregulate some fat oxidation signaling pathways, this does not translate to performance advantages.

**Health risk**: Raleigh et al. (2024, *EJSS*) found that 28% of elite endurance athletes used fasted training, associated with 44% incidence of bone stress injury. The risk-benefit ratio is unfavorable.

**Recommendation**: Do not train fasted for performance goals. Fuel appropriately for session demands. If fat oxidation adaptation is specifically desired (rare scenario), limit to easy Z1-Z2 sessions only and never for threshold/VO2max work.
